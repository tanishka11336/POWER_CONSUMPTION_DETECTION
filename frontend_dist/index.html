Industrial & Academic Specification on Deep Learning Power Consumption Forecasting

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & SYSTEM SPECIFICATION

This technical document provides the complete architectural, algorithmic, and operational specification for the Tetouan Smart Grid Deep Learning Power Consumption System. The objective of this project is to develop and deploy high-performance Deep Learning forecasting models to predict multi-zone electricity consumption across the city of Tetouan, Morocco, with micro-interval precision (10 minutes) and multi-hour lookahead horizons.

1. DATASET CHARACTERISTICS & SENSOR TOPOLOGY

The dataset captures complete 2017 power consumption telemetry from the Moroccan electric utility in Tetouan. The telemetry stream consists of 52,416 observations recorded at regular 10-minute intervals across 364 days.

  • Zone 1 (Downtown / Commercial Center): Average demand of 32,345 KW (Peak: 52,204 KW, 45.4% grid volume). Characterized by high diurnal variance with strong daylight administrative and business loading.
  • Zone 2 (Industrial & Manufacturing Corridor): Average demand of 21,043 KW (Peak: 37,409 KW, 29.5% grid volume). Demonstrates steady baseload demand driven by manufacturing operations and shift schedules.
  • Zone 3 (Residential & Coastal Tourism Belt): Average demand of 17,835 KW (Peak: 47,598 KW, 25.1% grid volume). Exhibits dual morning and evening domestic peaks and extreme summer seasonal sensitivity due to air conditioning.
  • Meteorological Drivers: Continuous sensor feeds of Ambient Temperature (3.25°C to 40.01°C), Relative Humidity (11.3% to 94.8%), Wind Speed (0.05 to 6.48 m/s), and Solar Diffuse Flux (0 to 1,163 W/m²).

2. FEATURE ENGINEERING & TEMPORAL WINDOWING

To prevent lookahead data leakage, a strict chronological train/validation/test split of 70% / 15% / 15% was applied. Feature engineering encompasses:

  • Cyclical Trigonometric Encodings: Sine and cosine transformations of Hour of Day, Month of Year, and Day of Week to preserve continuous temporal continuity.
  • Thermodynamic Interaction Proxies: Heat Index Proxy (Temperature × Humidity / 100) capturing human thermal discomfort and corresponding air conditioning loads.
  • Solar Irradiance Flux: Aggregate diffuse flux combining general diffuse flows and horizontal diffuse flows.
  • Sliding Input Tensor: Input temporal window $W = 12$ time-steps (representing the past 2 hours of telemetry) formatted into tensor shape $[B, 12, 17]$.

3. DEEP LEARNING ARCHITECTURES EVALUATED

Five distinct neural architectures were designed, trained, and benchmarked:

  • 1. Residual Deep MLP (ResNet-MLP): Top performing architecture ($R^2 = 0.9964$, MAPE $1.06\%$, MAE $640.9$ KW, Latency $0.004$ ms). Dual residual skip connections with Layer Normalization and GELU.
  • 2. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM): 2-layer stacked LSTM network ($R^2 = 0.9873$, MAPE $2.05\%$).
  • 3. Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU): 2-layer GRU with reset and update gates ($R^2 = 0.9836$, MAPE $2.68\%$).
  • 4. Temporal Transformer: Positional encoding layer coupled with a 4-head Multi-Head Self-Attention Transformer Encoder ($R^2 = 0.9824$, MAPE $2.48\%$).
  • 5. 1D-CNN + BiLSTM (Hybrid): 1D Convolutional filter banks with Batch Normalization piped into Bidirectional LSTM ($R^2 = 0.9751$, MAPE $3.34\%$).

4. VERIFICATION & OPERATIONAL RECOMMENDATIONS

For production deployment in electric utility dispatch centers:

  • Model Selection Recommendation: The Residual-MLP model provides the highest accuracy and lowest compute footprint for real-time SCADA integration.
  • Automated Anomaly Protocols: When forecast variance exceeds 10% MAPE or total load surges beyond 90,000 KW, automated alerts trigger demand-response protocols and spinning reserve ramp-up.
